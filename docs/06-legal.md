# 06 — License, ownership and trademark

Status: 2026-08-06. This file records the licensing decision, what is already implemented, and what
must still happen before Retropolis goes public. It is not legal advice — the items under
[Open before launch](#open-before-launch) need a lawyer.

## The decision

**AGPL-3.0-or-later**, sole rights holder **Sebastian Grundhöfer**, CLA required for every external
contribution.

Rationale, in order of weight:

1. **It keeps monetisation open.** The rights holder is not bound by the licence they grant. Retropolis
   can be sold as a hosted service and simultaneously offered under a paid commercial exception
   licence to companies that cannot adopt copyleft. This is the Sentry/GitLab pattern.
2. **It is the strongest defence against a closed-SaaS clone.** AGPL § 13 forces anyone who runs a
   modified Retropolis over a network to offer their users the source. A competitor either publishes
   their work or buys an exception licence. A permissive licence (MIT, as Scrumlr and Reflect use)
   gives that away for free.
3. **It matches the positioning.** `01-product-spec.md` sells "genuinely free" against a market that
   has retreated from free. A source-available licence (BSL, SSPL, FSL) would contradict the pitch.

**Not planned:** a later relicense away from copyleft. Published versions stay AGPL forever and the
community can fork the last free commit — HashiCorp → OpenTofu is the cautionary case. The revenue
model must work _with_ the AGPL, not around it.

### What the model can and cannot be

| Viable                                            | Blocked by AGPL                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hosted instance (free tier + paid team tier)      | Classic open core — a proprietary "Pro" module in the same process is a derivative work |
| Commercial exception licence (per § 2 of the CLA) | Shipping a closed plugin API that only paid builds unlock                               |
| Support, consulting, managed/self-host assistance | Withholding source from users of the hosted instance                                    |
| Trademark licensing (once registered)             | —                                                                                       |

## Implemented

| Item               | Where                                                      |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Licence text       | `LICENSE` (verbatim AGPL-3.0, 34 523 bytes)                |
| SPDX headers       | 90 first-party source files, REUSE-style two-line header   |
| Package metadata   | `license: AGPL-3.0-or-later` in all four `package.json`    |
| CLA                | `CLA.md` (German binding, English translation)             |
| Contribution rules | `CONTRIBUTING.md`                                          |
| CLA enforcement    | `.github/workflows/cla.yml` (contributor-assistant v2.6.1) |
| In-app notices     | `apps/web/src/components/LegalFooter.tsx` — see below      |

`apps/worker/worker-configuration.d.ts` deliberately carries no header — it is regenerated by
`wrangler types` and any header would be wiped.

### The in-app notices (AGPL § 5(d) and § 13)

`LegalFooter` renders on all three screens a participant actually uses — the home page, the join gate
and the board — and carries the four Appropriate Legal Notices that § 0 defines and § 5(d) makes
mandatory (copyright, the licence recipients may convey under, the warranty disclaimer, how to view
the licence) plus the § 13 source offer.

The source link points at the **exact running commit**. The SHA is injected at build time via
`VITE_COMMIT_SHA`, set in both deploy paths:

- `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` — `${{ github.sha }}` for production, the PR head SHA for previews
- `apps/web/package.json` `deploy` script — `$(git rev-parse HEAD)` for a local `wrangler deploy`

A build without that variable degrades to the default branch instead of the exact version. That is
correct for `pnpm dev` and for tests, but **a deployed build must always carry it** — verified by
building both ways.

**Caveat: the repository is still private, so both links currently 404 for anyone but the owner.**
The § 13 obligation is only actually discharged once the repo is public (or the source is served
some other way). Making it public is therefore a launch blocker, not a nice-to-have.

## Open before launch

1. **Make the repository public.** The in-app source offer is built and wired, but it points at a
   private repository — so as it stands the app promises source it does not deliver. Nothing else on
   this list is blocked by it, and it also fixes item 5.
2. **Have the CLA reviewed.** Two specific questions for the lawyer:
   - § 31a UrhG requires _Schriftform_ (wet signature) to grant rights for **unknown** future types of
     use. A click/comment signature cannot satisfy that, so § 2 of the CLA is deliberately limited to
     types of use known at the time of contribution. Confirm this limitation is acceptable, or decide
     whether high-value contributors should sign on paper.
   - The CLA is presented as standard terms, so it is subject to Inhaltskontrolle under § 307 BGB.
     Ask whether the exclusive grant in § 2 survives that review, and whether § 7's reversion clause
     is sufficient consideration.
3. **Register the trademark** — see below.
4. **Impressum and Datenschutzerklärung** (§ 5 DDG, Art. 13 GDPR). Separate from licensing, but
   required before the hosted instance is publicly reachable. See `05-privacy-gdpr.md`.
5. **GitHub Actions billing.** CI has not run since 2026-07-25 — every run fails with _"the job was
   not started because recent account payments have failed or your spending limit needs to be
   increased"_. The CLA workflow is therefore inert until this is fixed. Making the repository public
   also removes the problem: standard runners are free for public repos.

## Dependency policy

**The production closure must stay permissive (MIT / BSD / ISC / Apache-2.0).** A single copyleft
dependency in the core would make the commercial exception licence unsellable, because the rights
holder cannot grant an exception to code they do not own.

Audit of 2026-08-06 — 21 third-party packages in the production closure, **zero copyleft**:

| Licence    | Packages                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| MIT        | `@babel/runtime`, `@types/react`, `cookie`, `csstype`, `event-target-polyfill`, `hono`, `html-parse-stringify`, `i18next`, `partysocket`, `react`, `react-dom`, `react-i18next`, `react-router`, `scheduler`, `set-cookie-parser`, `use-sync-external-store`, `void-elements`, `zod`, `zustand` |
| ISC        | `canvas-confetti`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Apache-2.0 | `typescript`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

Apache-2.0 is one-way compatible: it may be combined into AGPLv3 work, but not the reverse. That is
fine here and does not affect the exception licence, since Apache-2.0 permits proprietary
sublicensing.

Note the planned `emoji-picker-element` (`01-product-spec.md`) is Apache-2.0 — acceptable. Re-run the
audit before each release:

```sh
pnpm -r list --prod --depth Infinity --json   # then check each package's license field
```

## Trademark "Retropolis"

### What was found

The name is **already in active commercial use** by at least three unrelated software products:

- _The Secret of Retropolis_ and _Retropolis 2: Never Say Goodbye_ — VR adventure games by Peanut
  Button Studio, sold on Steam and Meta Quest.
- _Retropolis – Rule the Streets_ — a mobile strategy game by VoidForge, on the App Store and Google
  Play (`retropolisgame.com`).

These sit in **Nice class 9 (software)** and class 41 (entertainment). A retro tool needs class 9 and
class 42 (SaaS). **Class 9 overlaps literally**, which is where a conflict would arise.

**This is not a clearance search.** The public trademark registers (DPMAregister, TMview, EUIPO
eSearch, WIPO Global Brand Database) are not machine-readable and Justia blocks automated access, so
whether any of these products hold _registered_ marks is **unverified**. Do not treat the absence of a
finding as a clean result.

### What to do, in order

1. **Free self-search first**, before spending anything:
   - DPMAregister — <https://register.dpma.de> (German marks)
   - TMview — <https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/> (DE + EU + international in one query)
   - EUIPO eSearch — <https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/search>
   - WIPO Global Brand Database — <https://branddb.wipo.int>

   Search classes 9, 41 and 42, and search for similar marks, not just the exact string —
   Verwechslungsgefahr covers similarity, not identity.

2. **If the register looks clear, still get a professional Recherche** (roughly €300–800) before
   filing. Self-searches routinely miss phonetically or conceptually similar marks, and filing into a
   conflict costs the fee plus an Abmahnung.

3. **File.** Verified fees as of August 2026:

   | Office     | Scope   | Basic fee                            | Extra classes                      | Term     |
   | ---------- | ------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------- |
   | DPMA (DE)  | Germany | **€290** electronic, up to 3 classes | €100 per further class             | 10 years |
   | EUIPO (EU) | All EU  | **€850** electronic, 1 class         | €50 for the 2nd, €150 each further | 10 years |

   Recommendation: **DPMA first** (classes 9 + 42, €290 total). The audience per
   `03-competitive-analysis.md` is German-speaking teams that want EU residency, so German protection
   covers the actual market at a third of the EU cost. Escalate to EUIPO only if the product goes
   international — a German registration gives a six-month priority window for that.

4. **If the name turns out to be blocked**, decide _now_, while the repo is still private and nothing
   is published. Renaming costs almost nothing today and is expensive after launch.

### Even without registration

Using "Retropolis" commercially in Germany creates an **Unternehmenskennzeichen** under § 5 MarkenG,
which gives limited protection from first use. That is weaker than a registered mark — it does not
survive a third party registering the name — but it means the name is not entirely unprotected in the
window before filing.

The trademark is worth more than the licence here: it is the one asset a fork cannot take. Anyone may
fork the AGPL code, but nobody may call their fork Retropolis. That is exactly how Grafana, Redis and
WordPress protect themselves.

## Why the CLA matters more than it looks

The moment an external pull request is merged **without** a signed CLA, that contributor becomes a
joint rights holder in their portion. From then on:

- no commercial exception licence can be sold that includes their code,
- no relicensing decision can be made unilaterally,
- enforcement against an infringer needs their cooperation.

There is no retroactive fix short of tracking every contributor down or ripping their code out. The
repository is private and has no external contributors yet, which is why this was set up now — it is
the cheapest it will ever be.
